Automate: five ways to rebuild it

Today the page is two tabs with three different ideas under them. A workflow, a routine and a task waiting for your limit to reset all mean "do this without me", and each sits in its own place with its own form. The page also shows how each one is set up, but almost nothing about what it did.

Each mockup below picks something else to lead with: time, plain sentences, results, what starts them, or what they cost. Your three routines are the real data. Two example workflows (Red build fixer, Downloads sorter) and one queued task stand beside them so the screens have something to show. Use the switch in the bar to see each mockup at panel size or full window.

What the screenshots show

  • One idea, two tabs. A routine is a workflow with a schedule and one Claude step, but the two are made, listed and edited apart.
  • The prompt is the label. Routine rows show the raw instruction cut off mid-sentence, with no plain summary of what the routine does.
  • Results are hidden. "Ran 4d ago" says it ran, not what it found. You have to open a tab to learn the tax check said nothing is due.
  • Eleven templates in a wall. The cards all look alike and their text is cut off, and on a wide screen they stretch into one long strip.
  • Leftovers at the bottom. Secrets, cloud routines and the limit-reset queue sit below the fold, each in its own style.
  • Wide screens are wasted. At 4K the input runs 1,900px wide and two thirds of the page is empty.

Tide chart

One page, no tabs. A tide line across the top shows the week: runs that already happened sit on the wave behind "now", and runs still to come wait ahead of it. The 5-hour limit window is shaded, so a task waiting for the reset sits where it will actually run.

Below the chart is one list of every automation, filtered by what starts it. Workflows that wait for a build or a file don't fit on a timeline, so they sit on the "Listening" line under the wave.

Fixes
Two tabs, three forms. "When your limit resets" stops being a separate form at the bottom and becomes a point on the chart.
Wide screen
The chart spans the full width, and the list gets a detail pane with the last result and run history.
Cost
Needs a single "next runs" feed that covers routines, scheduled workflows and the reset queue. Most of that data already exists in the routine and workflow schedulers.
Risk
Quiet weeks look empty. With only Monday jobs, most of the wave is bare.

Try: press a marker on the wave, switch to Next day, filter to Listening.

When, then

Making an automation becomes finishing a sentence. Every blank is a short menu: what starts it, what Claude does, where, and how you hear about it. Choosing a schedule makes a routine and choosing an event makes a workflow, so you never have to know which one you need.

Your automations are listed in the same sentence form, so "Every Monday at 8:30, Claude re-checks state PFAS rules" replaces a prompt cut off mid-word. The templates become starter sentences you can press to load and then change.

Fixes
Rows labelled with the raw prompt, the wall of templates, and having to pick between routine and workflow before you start.
Wide screen
The sentence is set large on the left, with the starters beside it. It reads like a form you fill in, not a long input bar.
Cost
Each automation needs a one-sentence summary. Claude can write it when the automation is drafted, and older ones fall back to their trigger and name.
Risk
Workflows with If branches don't fit in one sentence. The sentence covers the start; the map is still one click away.

Try: press any coloured blank, or one of the starter sentences.

Inbox

The page opens on what your automations said, not how they're set up. An Ask me step, a run that failed, or a routine paused for weeks goes to the top under "Needs you", with buttons that settle it in place. Under that, every finished run shows a few lines of what it found.

The list of automations becomes a column on the side. You switch things on and off there, and opening one takes you to its editor as it does today.

Fixes
"Ran 4d ago" with no result. You'd see "Nothing is due in the next 14 days" without opening a tab.
Wide screen
The inbox fills the middle and the list sits in a 340px column. At panel size the list moves under the inbox.
Cost
Each run keeps a short result excerpt: the final message of its Claude step or a Tell me step's text. Runs already end in a tab, so it's a matter of storing that text.
Risk
It overlaps with the Feed and notifications. Results would need to be marked read once you've seen them in either place.

Try: press Take a crack, or Turn it back on.

Switchboard

Automations are grouped by what starts them: the clock, your code, files and this PC, the limit reset, and things outside Shellby. Each group shows what's plugged in, and an empty one suggests the two templates that fit it. "Nothing listens to your builds yet" with Red build fixer under it is easier to act on than a wall of eleven cards.

It also shows what Shellby can do. Many people won't know a workflow can start when a dev server crashes or a test flakes until they see an empty plug for it.

Fixes
Template overload, and triggers you'd never find. Templates appear next to the thing they react to.
Wide screen
Five columns side by side, like a patch bay. At panel size they stack, and empty ones shrink to one line.
Cost
Small. It's a grouping of what exists, and the templates already know their trigger.
Risk
A workflow with two triggers shows up in two columns. That's honest, but it can look like a duplicate.

Try: press a suggestion in an empty column to plug it in.

Ledger

For when you have a dozen automations and need to know which ones earn their keep. Each row shows its last 14 runs as blocks, how long it usually takes, and how much of your plan it used this month. Sorting by "Most trouble" puts the flaky ones first.

Model advice moves out of the editor and into the row: a routine that has only made small runs on your top model gets a one-line note and a button to switch it to Sonnet.

Fixes
You can't tell what automations cost or whether they're working without opening each one.
Wide screen
A real table with sortable columns. At panel size each row folds to two lines with the run blocks under the name.
Cost
Usage per run, which Shellby already reads for tabs; the routine model hint already counts small runs.
Risk
It can feel like a spreadsheet. It works best as the second half of mockup 1 or 3, not as the whole page for a new user.

Try: sort by Most trouble, press a row, switch Downloads sorter to Sonnet.

They combine well. Mockup 2's sentences can label the rows in any of the others, mockup 4's empty-lane suggestions can replace the template wall, and mockup 5 makes a good "Details" view for mockup 1 or 3.
